Opinion: the electric bus plan has a winter problem

Marrowby Gazette · Comment · 20 February 2026

Nobody doubts the council's good intentions. Cleaner air and quieter streets are worth wanting. But before we cheer the plan to put 12 electric buses on the road next year, it is worth reading the small print — especially the part about cold weather.

The vehicle the council is looking at is quoted with a rated range of 280 km. That number is measured in mild conditions. An independent study of electric buses in cold weather found that winter range drops by about 30 percent, as heating the cabin and the battery eats into the energy that would otherwise move the bus. On a hard January morning, a bus rated for 280 km may manage closer to 200. On the town's longest loops, that is the difference between finishing the day and limping back for a midday top-up.

Then there is the money. An electric bus costs £465,000. A diesel one does the same job for £240,000 — not far off half the price. The council points to a national grant and to future savings on fuel and maintenance, and those savings may well be real. But they arrive years from now, and only if the buses perform as promised through every winter in between. Capital is spent up front; savings are a forecast.

None of this is an argument for standing still. It is an argument for honesty about the risks. The council should publish the winter range it actually achieves, route by route, in the first year of service, and set out what happens to the business case if the buses fall short. Ask the hard questions now, while there is still time to order a few more chargers or stagger the routes, rather than discovering the gap on the coldest day of the year with passengers waiting in the dark.
