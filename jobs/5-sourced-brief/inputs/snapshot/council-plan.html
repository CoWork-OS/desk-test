Cabinet report: electric bus transition programme

Reference TRA-2026-07 · Prepared for Cabinet by the Director of Environment and Transport

Summary

This report asks Cabinet to approve a programme to replace the town's diesel bus fleet with battery-electric buses. Marrowby Buses currently operates a fleet of 38 diesel buses across eight town routes. The programme sets a target of a fully electric fleet by 2029, delivered in three phases.

Phase 1

Phase 1 commits to an initial order of 12 electric buses for delivery in 2026, serving the three highest-mileage routes. These routes were chosen because they return to the Kestrel Lane depot overnight, where charging can be concentrated. Later phases, to be funded in future budget rounds, will replace the remaining vehicles through 2027–2029.

Costs

Each electric bus costs £465,000, against £240,000 for an equivalent diesel bus. The higher capital cost is partly offset by external funding. Marrowby has been awarded £6.2 million from the national clean-transport grant, which will be applied to the vehicle order and depot works. Converting the Kestrel Lane depot for overnight charging is estimated at £3.8 million, covering grid reinforcement, charging units and civil works.

Expected savings

Officers estimate savings of £1.1 million per year on fuel and maintenance once the whole fleet is electric, reflecting lower energy costs per mile and reduced servicing of simpler drivetrains. These savings are expected to accumulate from 2029 onward and form the core of the programme's long-term business case.

Recommendation

Cabinet is asked to: (1) approve the transition programme and its 2029 target; (2) authorise the Phase 1 order of 12 buses; and (3) approve the Kestrel Lane depot upgrade, subject to planning consent. A detailed budget breakdown is published separately by the Finance service.
