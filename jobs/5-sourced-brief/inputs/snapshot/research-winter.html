Cold-weather range of battery-electric buses

Independent study summary · Transit Research · published January 2026

Scope

This summary reports findings from an independent study of how ambient temperature affects the operating range of battery-electric city buses. The study tracked a mixed sample of low-floor single-deck buses in regular passenger service over one full year, comparing range achieved in mild months with range achieved in sustained cold.

Key finding

Across the sample, the study found that winter range drops by about 30% relative to the range achieved in mild conditions. In other words, a bus that comfortably meets its rated range in spring or autumn should be expected to deliver roughly two-thirds of that figure during a cold snap. The effect was consistent across vehicles from several manufacturers.

Why it happens

The study attributes most of the loss to cabin heating, which draws energy directly from the traction battery, and to reduced battery efficiency at low temperatures. Pre-heating vehicles while still connected to depot power — so the cabin and battery start the day warm — recovered part, but not all, of the lost range. Driving style and frequent door openings in cold weather contributed smaller additional losses.

Implications for operators

The authors caution that manufacturer rated range figures are typically measured in mild conditions and should not be used directly for winter scheduling. They recommend that operators plan routes against a winter range roughly 30% below the rated figure, build in depot pre-heating, and retain a contingency for the coldest days of the year. Operators converting long rural or semi-rural loops are advised to model the worst-case winter range explicitly before committing a route to a single overnight charge.

This is a plain-language summary; the full methodology and dataset are published separately.
