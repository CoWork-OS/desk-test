Council approves switch to electric buses

Marrowby Gazette · Local Democracy Reporter · updated 14 February 2026

Correction: an earlier version of this article said the grant was £9 million. The award is £6.2 million.

Marrowby town council has approved a plan to replace its entire diesel bus fleet with battery-electric vehicles, following a vote by the transport committee this week. The town currently runs 38 diesel buses, and councillors set a target of a fully electric fleet by 2029.

The first stage will see 12 electric buses ordered for delivery in 2026, concentrated on the busiest routes. Officers told the committee that the switch would cut running costs over time, projecting savings of £1.1 million per year on fuel and maintenance once every bus in the fleet is electric.

The programme leans heavily on outside money. Marrowby has secured £6.2 million from the national clean-transport grant, which will cover much of the vehicle order and the cost of rebuilding the Kestrel Lane depot so buses can charge overnight. The remainder comes from the council's own capital budget.

Not everyone was convinced. One councillor voted against, warning that the capital cost of electric buses — far above the price of a diesel equivalent — could squeeze other services if projected savings do not materialise. The chair said performance, including winter range, would be monitored once the first vehicles enter service, and that officers would report each year on actual against forecast savings.

A council spokesperson said the depot works were expected to finish before the first buses arrive, and that residents would see no change to timetables during the transition. Later phases, covering the rest of the fleet, will be funded in future budget rounds as the rollout continues route by route toward the 2029 deadline.
