Going electric — your questions answered

Marrowby Buses is switching to battery-electric buses. Here is what the change means for you as a rider.

What is changing?

We are replacing our fleet of 38 diesel buses with battery-electric buses. The town council has set a target for the whole fleet to be fully electric by 2029. The change will happen gradually, route by route, so most of the time you won't notice anything different except a quieter, smoother ride.

When do the first electric buses arrive?

The first 12 electric buses have been ordered for delivery in 2026. They will start on our busiest routes, which return to the Kestrel Lane depot overnight for charging. The remaining routes will follow in later phases through to 2029.

Will fares or timetables change?

No. Fares are not changing because of the switch to electric, and timetables will stay the same during the transition. If a specific route's schedule changes for any reason, we will publish the update in the usual way at stops and online.

Are electric buses reliable in winter?

Electric buses use more energy in cold weather, mainly to heat the cabin, which can reduce how far they travel on a charge. We have planned routes and overnight charging with that in mind, and we will monitor winter performance closely in the first year of service.

Why is the council doing this?

Electric buses are quieter and produce no exhaust emissions on the street. They also cost less to run: the council expects to save around £1.1 million per year on fuel and maintenance once the whole fleet is electric. Much of the up-front cost is being met by a national clean-transport grant.

Will accessibility features stay the same?

Yes. The new buses are low-floor with the same step-free boarding, wheelchair space and priority seating you rely on today.
