# Interview 08: Out-of-hours nurse coordinator, six-vet hospital

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-17. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about your role.

**Participant:** I coordinate overnight and weekend cover for a six-vet hospital in Dunmallow. Thirty-one staff, and eight of us rotate through nights. I've been doing nights in one form or another for nineteen years, so I've seen a lot of rota systems come and go. Tidemark's been with us six months.

**Interviewer:** How does on-call coverage work with Tidemark?

**Participant:** The planned rotation works well. It's the changes that hurt. Say a night vet's mother goes into hospital at four in the afternoon. Someone needs to approve the replacement, someone needs to update the on-call phone forwarding, and the reception team on the late shift needs to know who to page. Tidemark handles step one and leaves the other two to luck.

**Interviewer:** Who approves those swaps today?

**Participant:** Officially the clinical director. But she leaves at six, so after that it's whichever senior is around, and the app doesn't know that. It just sends the approval to her phone while she's at her daughter's football practice. I've watched a swap sit "pending" for five hours while the person covering was already in scrubs.

**Interviewer:** What's the impact of that?

**Participant:** Confusion more than danger, usually. But overnight, confusion is danger, because there are only two or three of us in the building. Approval rules should follow the clock, not the org chart.

**Interviewer:** You also mentioned the reception team needing to know.

**Participant:** Right, and this is the visibility part. The late receptionist looks at Tidemark and still sees the original vet's name, because the change is "pending." So a client calls about a collapsed dog, and she tells them Dr. Abara is on, and Dr. Abara is actually sitting in a hospital car park two towns over.

**Interviewer:** How do people find out what really happened?

**Participant:** They ask me the next morning. There's no trail anyone below manager level can see. And I think the team quietly resents that, because they're the ones who answer the phone when it goes wrong. Nurses will forgive a last-minute change; they won't forgive finding out about it from a client.

**Interviewer:** If Tidemark showed the history, what would it need to include?

**Participant:** Who requested it, who approved it, what time, and a one-line reason. That's four fields. And put them on the shift itself, not in some admin report. If I tap a night shift, I should see its whole story in one glance.

**Interviewer:** Have you found any workarounds?

**Participant:** We have a laminated sheet by the night phone where people write changes in marker. Six months into a digital scheduling tool and the most trusted record in the building is a laminated sheet.

**Interviewer:** Anything you want to add?

**Participant:** Only that nights are where a scheduling product proves itself. Days are easy; anyone can schedule a Tuesday at ten.
