# Interview 04: Receptionist lead, five-vet hospital

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-09. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** Thanks for joining. Tell me about your role.

**Participant:** Of course. I lead the front desk at a five-vet hospital in Saltweir, so I've got six receptionists under me, and I sort of became the Tidemark person by accident because I was the one who complained loudest about the old spreadsheet. We're about five months in, twenty-six staff in total.

**Interviewer:** What does the front desk use it for most?

**Participant:** Mostly checking who's on and swapping lunch covers. And honestly the main thing I hear from my team is, "Why did my shift move?" Somebody opens the app, sees their Wednesday has turned into a Thursday, and there's no note, no name, nothing.

**Interviewer:** Is there a history or a log?

**Participant:** There is, sort of, if you're an admin and you dig three menus deep. My receptionists can't see it. So they come to me, and I don't know either, so I go to the practice manager, and by then it's become a whole drama. A schedule that changes without a name on it feels like something being done to you rather than with you.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example?

**Participant:** Yeah, there was a week in July where Priyanka's late shift got moved three times. It turned out one of the vets had been dragging blocks around on the planning view, just experimenting, not realizing it saved live. She thought she was being punished for something. She actually cried in the staff room.

**Interviewer:** That's a hard moment.

**Participant:** It was. And the fix is so small. Just show "changed by Dr. Whoever, reason: covering surgery list" right on the shift. People don't mind change; they mind mystery.

**Interviewer:** How do people hear about changes now?

**Participant:** The phone notifications, in theory. But they buzz for literally every little edit, so half my desk switched them off within a month. I kept mine on for a while out of duty, and then I was getting buzzed at my niece's birthday party about a lunch break three weeks away. That was it for me, I turned them off too.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you wish Tidemark did that it doesn't do at all?

**Participant:** A payroll export. At the end of every month I sit with Tidemark open on one screen and our payroll spreadsheet on the other, copying hours across by hand for twenty-six people. It takes me most of a day. The hours are already in there; I just want a button that gives me a file our payroll person can actually use.

**Interviewer:** How much time would that save?

**Participant:** Six, seven hours a month, easily. And fewer mistakes, because I've definitely typed a 7.5 as 75 before. That was a fun phone call.

**Interviewer:** Overall, how do you feel about the product?

**Participant:** I like it more than I complain about it, which surprises people. But right now it knows everything and tells nobody the right thing.
