# Interview 05: Relief vet coordinator, mixed practice

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-10. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** Could you explain what a relief vet coordinator does at your practice?

**Participant:** Sure. We're a mixed practice outside Brindlecombe, small animal plus some farm work, and we lean heavily on locums. I keep a pool of about fourteen relief vets warm, meaning I know who's available, who does large animal, who refuses to do nights. My job is basically filling holes before the holes become emergencies.

**Interviewer:** How long have you used Tidemark for that?

**Participant:** Nine months. We moved over in December, which in hindsight was a terrible month to learn new software.

**Interviewer:** How does the pricing work out for a pool like yours?

**Participant:** That's my main gripe, so I'm glad you asked. We pay $10 per seat per month. Nineteen core staff, fine, no argument. But to schedule a relief vet in Tidemark they need a seat, and I've got fourteen of them. Most months, maybe five of them actually work for us.

**Interviewer:** So you're paying for people who aren't working.

**Participant:** I'm paying $90 a month to keep nine people on a list. I did the maths for our partners: across the year that's over a thousand dollars for relief vets who never set foot in the building. It's a per-seat model built by someone who's never had to run a locum pool.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried deactivating them between shifts?

**Participant:** Yes, and deactivating someone wipes their availability notes, so when I reactivate them I'm starting from scratch. So I can either pay for empty seats or redo admin every month. Neither feels like a choice a customer should have to make.

**Interviewer:** You also mentioned filling holes. How does Tidemark handle last-minute gaps?

**Participant:** Badly, if I'm being candid. When a vet calls in sick at seven in the morning, I need to offer that shift to three or four relief people at once and take whoever says yes first. Tidemark only lets me offer it to one person at a time. So I end up on the phone, which is exactly what I bought the software to stop doing.

**Interviewer:** What happens after someone accepts by phone?

**Participant:** Then I have to go back into Tidemark and enter it manually, and then message the clinical director separately, because the app doesn't tell her the gap was filled. Twice this summer she'd already rung around herself, so we ended up with two locums for one slot and paid both. That's $800 for a mistake the software could have prevented.

**Interviewer:** If you could redesign one thing, what would it be?

**Participant:** An open-shift broadcast: send it to everyone qualified, first yes wins, everyone else gets told it's gone, and the director sees it closed. Pair that with a relief seat that only bills in months when the person actually works, and I'd be a very happy customer.

**Interviewer:** And as things stand?

**Participant:** As things stand, I'd call it a good planning tool sold at a price that punishes flexible staffing. The irony is that flexible staffing is exactly why we needed a scheduling tool.
