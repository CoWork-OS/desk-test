# Interview 03: Head nurse, four-vet clinic

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-07. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** Can you start by telling me about your role and the clinic?

**Participant:** Yeah, so I'm head nurse at a four-vet clinic in Esterlow. Twenty-two staff, we do our own out-of-hours on a rotation, which is the bit that matters for this conversation, I think. Tidemark's been in for about seven months.

**Interviewer:** Why is the out-of-hours piece important?

**Participant:** Because that's where it falls apart. Planned shifts are fine. It's the 6 a.m. text that says "my kid's got a temperature, can someone cover" that the app doesn't really handle. Tidemark is great at the schedule you plan and pretty useless at the schedule you actually get.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through what happens in that situation.

**Participant:** Okay, so, say Jess can't do her on-call Saturday. She can post a swap request in the app, fine. But then who approves it? In theory it's me. In practice I'm in theatre with my hands inside a Labrador, so the request sits there, and meanwhile three nurses have seen it and nobody knows if it's taken. Last month we had two people turn up for the same on-call night and nobody for the next one.

**Interviewer:** How did you find out?

**Participant:** The vet on call rang the empty nurse phone at 11 p.m. and got voicemail. Nobody was hurt, thank goodness, but that was a long night for me. A swap that nobody confirmed is just a rumour with a timestamp.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the app to do instead?

**Participant:** If I don't approve within, I don't know, thirty minutes, kick it to the deputy. And tell the on-call vet directly, because right now the vet is the last person who hears. I'd love one big red "covered" or "not covered" for every on-call night, that's all I really want.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned seeing requests. How do people get notified?

**Participant:** Push notifications on their phones, and that's the other problem. It pings for everything. Someone edits a break by fifteen minutes, ping. A comment on Tuesday's lunch cover, ping. I get maybe forty a day. So I muted it in week two, and so did most of my team.

**Interviewer:** So when something important changes...

**Participant:** Exactly, we don't see it. Muting it was the only way to stay sane, and it's also the reason the on-call mix-up happened. The thing shouting at you all day is the same thing that went quiet on the one night it mattered.

**Interviewer:** Is there a setting to filter them?

**Participant:** There's an on-off switch per category, but the categories don't map to anything a nurse thinks about. I don't care about "schedule edits." I care about "my shift changed" and "on-call is uncovered." Give me those two and I'd turn notifications back on tomorrow.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want the product team to hear?

**Participant:** Just that we're not asking for more features. We're asking for fewer pings and one clear answer about who's on tonight.
