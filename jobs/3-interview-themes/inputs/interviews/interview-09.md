# Interview 09: Practice manager, equine and farm practice

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-21. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** Could you describe the practice and your role?

**Participant:** I manage a large-animal practice near Pemberwick. Mostly horses, some cattle and sheep, and a small-animal room on the side that keeps the lights on. Twenty-four staff, but that number moves around a lot, because lambing season and foaling season bring in extra hands. We've had Tidemark four months.

**Interviewer:** How did onboarding go?

**Participant:** Not brilliantly. Our practice software is Vetrolume, same as a lot of mixed practices round here, and the connection kept treating farm visits as if they were clinic appointments in a room. So a vet out on a call at a stud farm showed up as double-booked in Consult 2. It took about a month and four support tickets before the calendar stopped lying to us.

**Interviewer:** And the staff side of setup?

**Participant:** We have people with odd contracts. Seasonal, zero-hours, a retired vet who does Tuesdays when his knees allow it. The import only wanted "full-time" or "part-time," so I had to fudge half the team. I'd say setup cost me forty hours I didn't have during the busiest part of spring.

**Interviewer:** Let's talk about pricing. What are you paying?

**Participant:** $14 a seat, because we needed the plan with travel time between yards. Which is fine for the vets out on the road every day. But I've got lambing helpers who work six weeks a year. Paying fourteen dollars a month, every month, for a seasonal helper is daft, and nobody at Tidemark has explained to me why it isn't.

**Interviewer:** How have you handled that?

**Participant:** I archive them at the end of the season and re-add them in spring, and lose their history every time. It's ridiculous admin to avoid a ridiculous bill. Somebody at head office has priced this for clinics in towns where everyone's on a nice, tidy contract.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you need that the product doesn't do?

**Participant:** Get the hours out into payroll. Our bookkeeper, Moira, comes in on the last Thursday of the month and needs everyone's hours, including overtime for night calvings. Tidemark has every hour recorded, and there's no way to export it in a format she can use. She retypes it from screenshots I send her.

**Interviewer:** Screenshots?

**Participant:** Screenshots. Twenty-four people, a dozen screens. A payroll export would save her about five hours a month and save me the awkward conversation when she's typed someone's callout hours into the wrong week. If they built that one thing, I'd forgive them a lot of the onboarding grief.

**Interviewer:** Would you recommend Tidemark to a similar practice?

**Participant:** To a small-animal clinic, probably yes. To a farm practice, I'd say wait a year and let them learn that not all vets work in rooms.
