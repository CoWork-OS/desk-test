# Interview 07: Co-owner vet, two-vet exotics clinic

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-16. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** You're one of our newer customers. How long has it been?

**Participant:** Two months, give or take. I co-own a small exotics and small-animal clinic in Kestlewick with one other vet, nine staff in total. Lots of rabbits, a frankly alarming number of bearded dragons.

**Interviewer:** How was getting started?

**Participant:** Slower than I expected, and I'm usually the person who sets up the tech for everyone. The Orrinvet connection, that's our practice software, needed an API key from Orrinvet's side, and Orrinvet took nine working days to send it. Then Tidemark's side wanted the key in a format that Orrinvet doesn't actually produce.

**Interviewer:** How did you resolve it?

**Participant:** A support engineer at Tidemark eventually wrote a little workaround for us. Very nice of him. But nobody told me upfront that the integration depended on two companies talking to each other, and I spent three weeks thinking I was the one doing it wrong. The setup guide reads like it was written for a clinic where everything goes right on the first try.

**Interviewer:** And importing your team?

**Participant:** Nine people shouldn't be hard, right? But the import choked on our nurses' qualification fields, because exotics certifications aren't in their dropdown. So I had to add every person by hand and then re-tag them. It's twenty minutes of work that turned into a whole evening because of one missing option.

**Interviewer:** Now that you're live, what's the day-to-day like?

**Participant:** The schedule view itself is lovely, genuinely. But the notifications are a firehose. I installed it on my phone, and on the first Monday I got something like twenty-five alerts before lunch, because I was the one building the schedule and every single save notified me about my own edits.

**Interviewer:** Notifications about your own edits?

**Participant:** Yes! I was being told that I had changed a shift that I had just changed. I muted the app by Wednesday. And then the next week a nurse swapped her Friday with someone, and I didn't notice until Friday morning when the wrong person was holding the rabbit.

**Interviewer:** Did that cause problems?

**Participant:** Only mildly, the person who showed up was perfectly competent. But it's the principle. An alert system that trains you to ignore it is worse than having no alerts at all.

**Interviewer:** What would you want instead?

**Participant:** A daily digest for the small stuff, one push only when my own shift or something I'm responsible for changes. And never, ever notify me about something I did myself thirty seconds ago.

**Interviewer:** Overall, how would you rate the first two months?

**Participant:** Seven out of ten, but the first three weeks alone were maybe a three. If they'd fixed onboarding before selling me on it, I'd be telling you nine.
