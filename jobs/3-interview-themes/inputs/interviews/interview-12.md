# Interview 12: Office manager, feline-only clinic

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-29. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** Let's start with the basics. Where are you and what do you do?

**Participant:** I'm the office manager at a feline-only clinic in Hadderby. Two vets, three nurses, a couple of receptionists, eleven of us altogether if you count our groomer, who's technically freelance. We've been on Tidemark for ten months.

**Interviewer:** How was the setup period?

**Participant:** Long. Um, longer than I'd planned, let's say. We use Larchbook for appointments, and the first sync pulled in four years of old appointments and tried to schedule staff against them. For about two days, Tidemark thought our head vet was booked on shifts back to 2022.

**Interviewer:** How did that get fixed?

**Participant:** Their onboarding person had me disconnect it, delete everything, and start over with a date filter. Which was fine, except nobody mentioned the date filter existed the first time. Then the staff import rejected our groomer and both receptionists because their email addresses were on a shared clinic account. All in, I think setup ate about three weeks of evenings.

**Interviewer:** And once it was running?

**Participant:** Day to day, it's much calmer than our old shared calendar. We pay $6 a seat on an early deal, so the money isn't my worry.

**Interviewer:** What about short-notice changes?

**Participant:** Oh, that's still hard. We're tiny, so if one nurse is off sick, everybody feels it. The swap button exists, but it doesn't tell anyone who's actually allowed to say yes. Last month our two vets both approved different nurses for the same Monday, so both nurses came in and one of them went home unpaid and pretty annoyed.

**Interviewer:** How did you sort that out?

**Participant:** I paid her for the morning out of goodwill and wrote a note on the fridge saying only I approve swaps now. Which is a bit backwards, isn't it, using a fridge note to fix a scheduling app.

**Interviewer:** How do people get told about changes?

**Participant:** Well, here's the irony. The phone alerts go off constantly, for things like someone viewing the rota or adding a comment, so both vets turned them off within the first month. Then the one important alert, that a nurse had dropped her Saturday, went to two phones that were silenced. I found out at 8:40 on Saturday with a waiting room full of cats.

**Interviewer:** That sounds stressful.

**Participant:** It was, a bit. I'm learning that a notification nobody reads might as well be a notification nobody sent. If they let me mark some changes as "urgent," with a different sound, I think people would turn the app back on.

**Interviewer:** Last question: what's one thing you'd want Tidemark to hear?

**Participant:** That small clinics aren't simpler, they're just thinner. When one person misses one message, there's nobody behind them to catch it.
