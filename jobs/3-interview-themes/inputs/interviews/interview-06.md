# Interview 06: Practice manager, two-site group

Fictional interview for the Desk Test. Date: 2026-09-14. Interviewer: R. Okafor.

**Interviewer:** You've been with Tidemark longer than most people I've spoken to. How long exactly?

**Participant:** Fourteen months. We were one of the early clinics, I think, back when the onboarding was a shared document and a lot of goodwill. We run two sites, one in Tamsley Vale and a smaller satellite in Ottercombe Down, thirty-eight staff across both.

**Interviewer:** How did the setup go back then?

**Participant:** Painfully. The Larchbook integration wasn't really an integration yet; it was a nightly file that sometimes arrived. And the staff import couldn't handle people who work at both sites, so eleven of our team appeared twice, as two different people, with two different holiday balances. Untangling that took me most of a month, and I'm not exaggerating for effect.

**Interviewer:** Did that improve?

**Participant:** It did, eventually. But I'd warn any group practice: budget six weeks for setup, not six days. The software assumes every clinic is one building, and we're not.

**Interviewer:** Once you were up and running, what changed for you?

**Participant:** The rota templates changed my working week. I've got templates for each site, a summer version, a "two vets on leave" version, and I can roll a whole month out in one go. I used to spend a full day a week on the rota; now it's closer to two hours. That's six hours back every single week.

**Interviewer:** What do you do with those six hours?

**Participant:** Actual management, for once. Appraisals, recruitment, the bits of the job I was hired for. If the templates disappeared tomorrow I'd cancel the subscription the same afternoon, because they're the only reason the numbers work for us.

**Interviewer:** Are there areas where it still falls short?

**Participant:** Transparency. With two sites, people get moved between buildings, and they want to know who decided that and why. At the moment a nurse just sees her Monday has jumped from Tamsley to Ottercombe, which is a forty-minute drive, with no explanation attached.

**Interviewer:** How does your team react to that?

**Participant:** Suspiciously, and I don't blame them. One of our senior nurses asked me, half joking, whether the app was "deciding things on its own." When people can't see the reason for a change, they invent one, and the invented reason is always worse than the real one.

**Interviewer:** Is there a change log you could point them to?

**Participant:** There's an audit trail, but only managers can open it, and it lists changes as code-like entries rather than sentences. What I'd like is a little "why" box I'm forced to fill in whenever I move someone, visible to that person. I'd even accept being nagged about it.

**Interviewer:** Overall, would you renew?

**Participant:** Already have, for another year. Fix the visibility and I'd stop apologising to my team for decisions the app made look sneaky.
